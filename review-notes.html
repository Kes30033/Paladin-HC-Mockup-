Review notes

What changed, where everything went, and what needs a decision from Paladin. This page is for review and would not be part of the public site.

Same content, far fewer places to look

12 to 7header links, in one bar in place of two
32 to 14footer links
7 to 1pages for documents, spec sheets, and videos
143 to 1pages for project photos, now one filterable gallery

What makes it simpler

  • Four top tasks sit right under the home page banner: find a product, get a document, see it installed, find my rep.
  • Tools come first. Tall banners and “scroll down” arrows are gone, so filters and results show as soon as a page opens.
  • Every product card links straight to its spec sheet.
  • Reps are found by state. Everyone’s phone and email are visible without hovering.
  • The same document no longer lives on several pages under different names. The nav called it “Architectural Documents” while the footer called it “Product Documents.”

Everything was kept

  • 294 products and all 271 spec sheet PDFs
  • 67 documents and catalogs, plus 21 videos
  • 143 project photos in all 11 room types
  • 216 client list entries and 18 team members
  • 16 articles, 20 trade shows, 4 CEU courses, and the 12-point company timeline

These counts come from a crawl of the live site on November 6, 2026, and were checked against the mockup’s data files.

Problems found on the live site

Each one was checked on the live site on November 6, 2026. These are worth fixing even if the redesign does not go ahead.

  • A footer link is dead on every page.
    “Informative Videos” goes to /quick-tips, which returns a 404.
  • Catalog filters hide most of the products they name.
    “Modular / AMICO” shows 2, but about 60 parts fit Modular or AMICO. Most of them are labeled “Evolution Rail Accessories.” “Hill-Rom Accessories” shows 0, while 27 parts fit Hill-Rom and most of them are labeled “Hill-Rom Rail Accessories.” Four other filters also show 0: Accessories for Modular & Hill-Rom or Wittrock & AMICO, Security Headwall, Carts, and Mobile Equipment Transport Systems.
  • The sitemap is out of date.
    5 listed product URLs return a 404 (for example /product-items/sm301-0-75-pm), and 8 live products are not listed, which can keep them out of search engines.
  • Product photos are very large.
    They average about 1 MB and run up to 4.1 MB each, and only 47 of 294 have smaller versions, which is 305 MB of images across the catalog. That is slow on phones and hospital networks. The mockup uses small thumbnails made from the same photos.
  • Product Overview buttons repeat themselves.
    “View Portfolio” and “View Catalog” both go to the same product page as “View Product.”
  • The Lunch & Learn form leaves out a course.
    Four courses are listed, but the form offers three, and the text says “two” presentations. Course files labeled “PDF” are Word documents.
  • The privacy policy has an incorrect link.
    The text shows paladinhc.com but the link goes to uici.com, an unrelated domain. It also refers to a Cookie Policy that is not on the site. Worth having counsel look at this. The mockup keeps the wording and only corrects the link.
  • Other links and buttons go nowhere.
    On Product Overview, 5 of the 6 “Contact To Place An Order” buttons link to “#”. So do the category link at the top of product pages and an extra social icon in the footer. “Back to Articles” is plain text on all 16 articles. The share icon on document cards opens a blank email, without the document link.
  • Some links land on a generic page.
    The three Hill-Rom “View Catalog” buttons and the “VA / DHA Catalog” link on Product Overview open the full, unfiltered catalog. “View Portfolio” on a product page opens the full portfolio.
  • Portfolio photos open a dead end.
    Each of the 143 photos opens in a new tab on a page with only the image: no menu, caption, or way back. All 143 also have blank alt text, so screen readers get no description.
  • Two products cannot be found with catalog filters.
    AC101-Swivel-Hill-VertAdjust and DEM160-PM have no catalog or type label. Legacy Series has no type label, and six products have no category.
  • The X (Twitter) link opens a login screen.
    It points to a login flow, not the Paladin profile.

What makes the site hard to use

  • Two nav bars and a long footer.
    12 header links are split across two bars, with 32 more in the footer.
  • Overlapping pages.
    The catalog and specification sheet pages largely repeat each other, and a second spec sheet page (/product-specification-sheets2) is still live. The nav uses one and the footer and product pages use the other.
  • Documents are scattered and named inconsistently.
    7 pages hold documents, spec sheets, and videos. 70 document links point to 64 files, and “Most Flexible Product Available in the Industry” exists as two different files. “Architectural Documents” in the nav is “Product Documents” in the footer, “Installation Guides” is “Helpful Guides,” and “Application Documents” is “Informational Documents.”
  • The contact page has no contact form.
    “Contact To Place An Order” leads to a page with email links only.
  • Rep details are hidden and hard to match to a state.
    Phone and email appear only on hover, and there is no way to search by state. Headquarters staff and regional reps are mixed together. NC, PA, IL, MO, and KS each list two reps with little or no explanation of who to call.
  • Clicks pile up browser tabs.
    Every product in the catalog opens in a new tab, and so do the contact buttons on product pages.
  • Tall banners push content down.
    Inner pages open with a large banner and a “scroll down” arrow before the tools. The About page is 11,698px tall on a desktop screen.

Content to confirm

  • No rep is listed for Texas, Arkansas, West Virginia, or western Ohio.
    The finder sends those states to the main office. If someone covers them, add them to the team data.
  • The trade show calendar needs 2027 dates.
    As of November 6, 2026, 19 of the 20 listed shows have passed. The mockup hides past shows automatically. The Kentucky Society show is listed twice, on Sept 22 to 24 and Oct 27 to 29, at the same venue. Please confirm which is right.
  • Elevation sheets could not be found.
    A hidden footer link and hidden buttons on product pages point to elevation sheets, but /elevations-sheets returns a 404 and no product has one. If they exist, they should be added to the library.
  • Two catalog editions are linked.
    The home page PDF is May 2025. Everywhere else is July 2025. The mockup features July and keeps May in the library.
  • Contact details disagree.
    The Middle East phone shows +1 407-614-8781 but dials +971 50 508 3344 on the Sales Team page. The Middle East address in the footer is the Oakland, FL address, so the mockup omits it. The hours are assigned on the assumption that 8 to 5 is headquarters and 7 to 3:30 is the warehouse.
  • Small copy conflicts.
    ISO 9001 on About but ISO9000 on Hill-Rom. “Ernst” and “Ernest” Schindele both appear. The footer says © 2024. The company appears as Paladin Healthcare, HealthCare, and Health Care. The rail appears as ONE Rail, ONE-Rail, and One Rail, and its compatibility list varies by page. The JSN Typicals and Item List cards repeat the Cross Reference Guide’s description.
  • Product labels are inconsistent.
    Labels include “Evolution Rail Accessories” and “Evolution Rail & Accessories,” “Hill-Rom Rail Accessories” and “Hill-Rom & Accessories,” and “Modular / AMICO,” “AMICO,” “AMICO VERTICAL CHANNEL,” and “Modular Rail Accessories.” AC101-PM-Hillrom Vert is labeled as an Evolution accessory.
  • Articles have no dates.
    None of the 16 show a publish date, so readers cannot tell what is current.
  • The privacy policy reads like a template.
    It mentions credit card details and registering for an account, though the site has no checkout or accounts. It also says the site is intended for users in the U.S.A., while Paladin lists offices in Europe and the Middle East. Worth including in the counsel review.
  • Likely typos, left as they are for you to approve.
    Client list: Attania, Leo A Daily, John’s Hopkins, Lucille Packard, Brigham and Woman’s, Eastern Main, Dusquesne, San Louis. The list also shows a “U.S. Navy Hospital” at Ft. Hood, which is an Army post. Documents: “Installation Manuel,” “Bed Locater.” Timeline: “pre-curser.” The Partners intro on the live site also has “the current and fu needs,” which this mockup left out.
  • Hidden template text is still in the page source.
    Visitors cannot see it, but search engines can read it: “Redefining your enduro ride” and earbud photos on ONE Rail, “Lowest mileage” sort options on Portfolio, and Lorem ipsum on product pages. The ONE Rail page has 7 main (H1) headings, none visible when the page loads. None of it was carried over.

Decisions made for this mockup

  • “Fits your rail” is new.
    I worked it out from part numbers and descriptions so people can filter by the rail on their wall. Someone who knows the product line should check it.
  • Six products had no category.
    I placed DEM160-PM, IM501, IM601, MC101, MC102, and SM501-4-K, and moved Revolution Series under Headwalls. Please confirm.
  • Rewritten copy.
    Product, document, team, and policy text comes straight from the live site. Page intros, button labels, and a few shortened About and Systems passages are new, and the comparison table was assembled from facts on the live site. All of it needs approval.

Before this could go live

  • Connect the contact and newsletter forms, and review the privacy policy since they collect personal details.
  • Move the full-size product photos and PDFs off the current file host. They still link to the live Webflow site, so they would break if that site is shut down.
  • Give each product and article its own real web address. They load from a single template here, which is fine for review but not ideal for search engines.
  • Set up redirects from the old addresses, remove the noindex tags, and remove this page and the footer review notice.
